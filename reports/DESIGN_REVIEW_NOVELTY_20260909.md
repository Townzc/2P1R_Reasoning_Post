# Task construction and novelty review after E010

Date: 2026-09-09. Status: bounded primary-source review and unexecuted design recommendations. No task generation, model inference, GPU work, or holdout access was performed for this review. This document does not authorize a new experiment or establish novelty.

E010 does not justify scaling: matched greedy final-answer accuracy was Paths 7/64 versus GCM 5/64, complete verified traces were 4/64 in both arms, and broader accuracy was 0/64 versus 2/64. These are the reported fixed-pool, single-pair outcomes, not evidence that the effect is zero. See [the result report](ABSENT_BOUNDARY_SEED31_RESULTS.md) and [the earlier positioning review](ICLR_POSITIONING_20260909.md).

The strongest available direction is a precisely limited study of **how training allocates interchangeable evidence routes, and whether that allocation affects use of the surviving evidence after intervention**. A new synthetic generator, several valid paths, or matched token counts would not by themselves support an ICLR contribution. Four automorphic routes that implement the same operation are not four semantic strategies.

## What the S5 transport candidate would actually test

The current unimplemented candidate assigns hidden independent uniform potentials \(h_v\in S_5\). An edge \(u\to v\) exposes the permutation table \(h_v\circ h_u^{-1}\). Given state \(x\) at source \(s\), the answer is \((h_t\circ h_s^{-1})(x)\). Four internally vertex-disjoint length-four paths connect the same endpoints. They all use the same forward composition operation and agree by telescoping. These properties are mathematical consequences of the proposal, not results of a generator audit.

This supplies four different sets of evidence inside an instance, while retaining a single algorithm and symmetric path skeletons. Forward/backward descriptions are execution or notation choices, not independently established strategies. An appropriate term is **evidence-route allocation**. A causal dependency on different edge instances is meaningful even when the paths are structurally interchangeable, but it must not be relabeled semantic strategy diversity.

For an intervention retaining \(r\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) routes, choose the surviving subset uniformly before any model output and remove a predeclared-uniform internal edge from each excluded route. The label remains unchanged and a route remains available. At \(r=4\), every branch succeeds; only deletion creates dead ends. Thus baseline branch confidence is not directly comparable to a task where only one branch originally reaches the target.

The Forks paper's graph target lies on a unique branch. Its A.5 intervention reorders dependency statements, and A.2 adds a branch-selection rationale. The proposed deletion instead changes which proofs exist while retaining the answer. That is an operational distinction from those specific controls. It remains closely related to the paper's concern about brittle route commitment; this review establishes neither priority nor a new mechanism. [Forks, A.1–A.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17026v2)

The falsifiable target could be: **at a fixed inventory of training questions and a fixed global route-exposure budget, distributing supervision across evidence routes changes complete-proof success under predeclared evidence loss, beyond its effect on intact-question performance and irrelevant-evidence loss**. Competing explanations include ordinary data augmentation, output-template learning, reduced prompt length, exposure to more edge tables, and generic tolerance of damaged inputs. These must be represented by controls, not dismissed in interpretation.

## Construction and control gates before any training

| Risk | Required CPU specification and audit | What a pass would and would not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planted proofs hide easier or inconsistent solutions | Independently solve the exposed graph and permutation tables. Enumerate bounded simple paths, check every composed result, shortest distance, route count, and every deletion view. Verify that potentials are hidden and no direct source-to-target table or endpoint value is emitted. Fix whether reverse traversal/inversion is legal. | Guarantees for planted routes alone do not characterize the legal solution set. Agreement does not establish computational difficulty. |
| Answer and construction shortcuts | Prove marginal answer balance for fixed query state; empirically inspect conditional balance by route count, statement order, node degree, table statistics and deletion location. Define cheap text/statistic baselines and a one-hop/partial-evidence test with exact oracle labels. | Uniform answers alone do not rule out conditional cues. A graph-only classifier should not predict one of five output states beyond its declared null. |
| Deletion changes input length or degree cues | Predeclare matched irrelevant-evidence deletion with the same removed-statement count, position distribution and appropriate distractor scaffold. Keep clean relabel/order perturbations as separate controls. Audit the scaffold for accidental routes. | A gain confined to shorter prompts or disconnected-statement patterns is not evidence of better use of alternative proofs. Some connectivity cues are legitimate; they cannot substitute for checking the composed answer and full trace. |
| Isomorphism or world leakage | Group all queries, node renamings, table renderings and deletion views from one latent world before splitting. Check canonical query-marked, edge-labeled graph duplicates, including declared state-label symmetries. Do not hash hidden potentials alone: common right-composition leaves all edge tables unchanged. | All four-chain bare topologies are isomorphic. New sampled potentials establish at most new labeled-world generalization, not topology or rule-composition OOD. An overly broad quotient under arbitrary local state changes can also collapse the intended task family; define the equivalence relation explicitly. |
| Synthetic path count mistaken for strategy count | Record evidence-edge sets, verified computations, automorphism classes and rendering classes separately. Check that distinct routes share no internal edges. Use path permutations and alternate surface renderings as negative controls. | Four evidence supports do not imply four algorithms. Associativity, traversal order, renaming, or an output skeleton must not inflate semantic coverage. |
| Supervision allocation remains confounded | Freeze the common prompt pool. Match per-question examples, updates and supervised tokens; counterbalance global edge-table/state-transition exposure and route positions. Distinguish intended per-question evidence exposure from incidental global imbalance. Audit the final tokenized manifests. | A balanced global count of anonymous route IDs is insufficient if IDs have no common semantic meaning or token/state marginals differ. Exactness must be defined on features that survive the generator's symmetries. |
| Grader accepts unsupported answers | Validate every cited edge in the intervened prompt, compose its permutation in the legal direction, and require a complete route to the requested target. Report invalid use of a deleted edge and incomplete traces separately. | Correct output state by guess or memorized answer is not evidence recovery. Local valid steps alone are also insufficient. |

Evidence deletion is not the same as changing the ground-truth function, nor is it an explanation-faithfulness test by itself. The correct answer is deliberately invariant. A model can retain the answer while giving a nonexistent proof, so the complete trace metric is indispensable.

## Decision and stopping rules

1. **Proceed only to a bounded CPU design audit now.** Freeze the generator, symmetry relation, deletion distribution, null baselines, verifier and allocation manifests before producing a training pool. Report failures and acceptance rates by every construction cell. Do not repeatedly reject instances until matching succeeds without auditing the resulting distribution.
2. **Abandon the semantic-strategy framing for the present S5 design.** Its defensible unit is interchangeable evidence support. To claim distinct strategies later would require a different, independently specified operation-level intervention and additional evidence; changing names or forward/backward skeletons is insufficient.
3. **Do not start GPU work if the result is only “a new multipath benchmark.”** The intended paper claim needs a prespecified interaction with evidence availability and a falsifiable competing explanation. Passing CPU checks establishes feasibility, not an ICLR contribution. Any training request also needs a separate budget decision.
4. **If trained later, make complete-proof performance on paired deletion views central.** Analyze all predeclared views, including intact and matched irrelevant-deletion controls, without selecting failures of one arm after seeing outputs. Keep views nested within worlds and training seeds separate; neither repeated views nor more renamed graphs replace independent training seeds.
5. **Pivot or report a narrow negative finding if the apparent benefit is only final-answer accuracy, disappears under world-grouped evaluation, follows prompt-size controls, or lacks the intended interaction.** A positive result only on the fixed four-chain template supports an evidence-routing boundary result. A broader claim would require independently specified task families or composition/topology shifts and replication, not another favorable slice of the same generator.

The immediate scientific deliverable is an auditable identification argument and generator rejection test, not a forecast of acceptance. This review identifies a potentially interpretable experiment while finding substantial overlap with existing allocation, graph-planning and proof-supervision work.
